Fat Loss, Results & Nutrition
Yes. Fat loss is primarily driven by a calorie deficit — meaning you consume slightly fewer calories than you burn. This can be achieved through a combination of home workouts, increased daily movement and practical nutrition changes. Many kmarfitness clients achieve significant fat loss training entirely at home with minimal equipment. A gym can be helpful but is by no means essential.
A healthy, sustainable rate of fat loss is typically 0.5–1kg per week, or roughly 2–4kg per month. This varies depending on starting weight, consistency, nutrition and individual metabolism. Kayleigh sets realistic, honest expectations from the outset — she won't promise you results she can't deliver. The goal is fat loss that lasts, not a quick drop that comes back. Clients who commit consistently over 12–16 weeks often see transformative changes.
No. kmarfitness does not advocate restrictive diets, food elimination or calorie obsession. Instead, Kayleigh provides practical nutrition guidance built around real life — helping you make better choices without feeling deprived. The focus is on sustainable habits: eating in a way that supports your goals while still enjoying the foods you love. There are no banned foods and no guilt-based approaches.
Fat loss coaching at kmarfitness is an evidence-based, whole-person approach that combines personalised training programming, practical nutrition guidance and sustainable lifestyle habits. Unlike a diet plan, coaching addresses the psychological, behavioural and physical aspects of fat loss — helping you understand why your body responds as it does, building habits that last long after the programme ends, and providing accountability through the moments when motivation dips.
Yes. All kmarfitness packages include practical nutrition guidance covering eating habits, meal timing, protein intake and calorie awareness. Kayleigh is not a registered dietitian, so she doesn't provide clinical dietary advice for medical conditions — but she provides evidence-based, practical nutrition coaching that helps most clients make significant improvements to their eating habits in a sustainable, non-restrictive way.
Cardio can support fat loss but is not strictly necessary — particularly for beginners. Resistance training (weights or bodyweight) builds muscle, which increases your resting metabolic rate over time. For most kmarfitness clients, a combination of resistance training, a moderate calorie deficit and increased daily activity (walking, steps) is a highly effective approach to fat loss without requiring long cardio sessions.
For fat loss, 3–4 training sessions per week combined with increased daily activity is an effective and manageable approach for most people. However, consistency over time matters far more than training frequency. Two consistent sessions per week for six months will outperform four sessions per week for four weeks. Kayleigh will recommend a frequency that is genuinely sustainable for your lifestyle.
Sports Massage
Sports massage is a targeted therapeutic massage technique designed to relieve muscle tension, support recovery and improve performance. Using a combination of pressure, friction, stretching and mobilisation techniques, sports massage helps reduce delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS), improve circulation, release adhesions in muscle tissue, restore range of motion and accelerate recovery after training or injury.
Not at all. Despite the name, sports massage is suitable for anyone — including people who don't exercise at all. Many of Kayleigh's sports massage clients are desk workers with chronic neck and back tension, people recovering from everyday injuries, or individuals managing long-term postural issues. If you have muscle tightness, soreness or restricted movement, sports massage is likely to benefit you.
Some discomfort during deeper work on tight or knotted areas is normal and expected — often described as a "good hurt" or productive discomfort. Kayleigh will always check in on pressure levels and adjust to your tolerance. You should never feel sharp, acute pain during a massage. Some clients experience mild soreness for 24–48 hours after a deep tissue session, which is normal and part of the recovery process.
For regular exercisers, a monthly sports massage is a good baseline for maintenance. If you're in heavy training, recovering from an injury or have a specific issue to address, more frequent sessions (every 2–3 weeks) may be beneficial. Kayleigh will advise on frequency based on your training load, goals and how your body responds after the first session.
Relaxation (Swedish) massage uses gentle, flowing strokes primarily to reduce stress and promote relaxation. Sports massage uses deeper, more targeted techniques — including trigger point therapy, cross-fibre friction and muscle manipulation — to address specific muscular issues, restore function and support recovery. Sports massage is purposeful and therapeutic rather than purely relaxing, though many clients find it deeply relaxing too.
Yes, in many cases. Sports massage can be highly effective for back pain caused by muscle tension, poor posture, overuse or stress. Kayleigh works on the muscles, fascia and soft tissue surrounding the spine to release tension and restore movement. If your back pain has a complex or clinical cause, you may also need input from a physiotherapist or GP — Kayleigh will advise honestly if that's the case.

Strength & Training Programmes
Progressive overload is the principle of gradually increasing the demands placed on your muscles over time — through adding weight, increasing reps, reducing rest time or improving technique. It's the fundamental mechanism by which the body gets stronger, fitter and more capable. Without progressive overload, training plateaus. kmarfitness programmes are built around this principle, ensuring clients continuously improve and never get stuck at the same level indefinitely.
For beginners, 2–3 training sessions per week is the ideal starting point. This gives the body enough stimulus to adapt and improve while allowing adequate recovery time between sessions. As fitness improves and recovery becomes more efficient, sessions can gradually increase. Kayleigh will assess your readiness and design a schedule that challenges you without overloading you — particularly in the first few weeks.
Active recovery on rest days — light walking, stretching, yoga or low-intensity movement — is generally beneficial and does not interfere with recovery. Intense training every day without rest, however, can lead to overtraining, fatigue and injury. Kayleigh programmes rest days into every client's schedule deliberately and will advise on how to use them productively.
An effective strength training programme for women typically includes compound movements (squats, hinges, presses and rows) targeting all major muscle groups, 2–4 times per week, with progressive overload applied over time. kmarfitness programmes for women are built around these principles, adapted to your goals, equipment and fitness level. Strength training is one of the most powerful tools available for improving body composition, bone density, energy levels and confidence in women of all ages.
Yes — to a meaningful degree, particularly for beginners. Bodyweight exercises like push-ups, squats, lunges, rows and hip hinges are highly effective for building strength and muscle when performed with proper technique and progressive overload. For more advanced clients seeking maximum muscle development, eventually adding external resistance (bands, dumbbells, a barbell) becomes beneficial — but significant progress is absolutely achievable with home-only training.
